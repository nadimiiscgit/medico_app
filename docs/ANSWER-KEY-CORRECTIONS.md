# Answer keys worth double-checking

Every entry here is a place where the study material disagrees with the
answer key or the explanation stored against a previous-year question.

This list exists because the corpus is not authoritative. Roughly 41% of its
explanations are machine-written, and every paper from 2025 onward is a
memory-based recall reconstructed by students — so its keys are evidence,
not fact. Learning a wrong key confidently is worse than skipping the
question, which is why each disagreement is written down rather than
silently resolved.

**These are claims to verify, not corrections to trust.** Check anything here
against your own textbook before you rely on it — particularly doses,
numerical cut-offs and national programme figures.

## Anatomy

### Oral Cavity, Tongue, Palate & Temporomandibular Joint

- **`neetpg-2019-s1-q0010`** (NEET PG 2019) — *Structure derived from pharyngeal arch:*  
  Stored key: **D. Anterior belly of digastric**
  
  **Disagreement with the supplied key, neetpg-2019-s1-q0010.** The question asks which structure is 'derived from pharyngeal arch' and the key gives the anterior belly of digastric. In fact ALL four options are arch derivatives - levator palatini is fourth arch, buccinator and stylohyoid are second arch, anterior belly of digastric is first arch. The stem as reproduced is defective and almost certainly asked about the FIRST arch, for which the key is right. Learn the arch of all four.

- **`inicet-2026-s1-q0003`** (INI CET 2026) — *Which muscle, as shown in the diagram, attaches to the cartilaginous portion of the Eustachian tube and also blends with the soft palate?*  
  Stored key: **C. Levator veli palatini**
  
  **Nuance in the supplied key, inicet-2026-s1-q0003.** Levator veli palatini is the right answer for a muscle descending from the medial lamina into the palate, but BOTH tensor and levator arise from the tube cartilage (tensor from the lateral lamina), and it is the tensor, not the levator, that actually dilates the tube on swallowing. Do not carry away the idea that levator is the tubal dilator.

## Forensic Medicine

### Identification & Forensic Anthropology

- **`neetpg-2012-s1-q0660`** (AIPGMEE 2012) — *DNA finger printing cannot be taken from ?*  
  Stored key: **B. Tooth**
  
  **Disagreement with the supplied key, neetpg-2012-s1-q0660.** The key says DNA fingerprinting cannot be obtained from a TOOTH. This is factually wrong. Dental pulp is one of the most reliable sources of nuclear DNA in charred, buried or skeletonised remains precisely because enamel and dentine protect it, and teeth are routinely used in mass-disaster victim identification. The buccal swab is merely the most convenient reference sample in a living person, not the only possible source. If this stem reappears, the intended answer is 'tooth' but the science is against it.

- **`neetpg-2013-s1-q0662`** (AIPGMEE 2013) — *Length of tibia is ?*  
  Stored key: **B. 20% of height**
  
  **Internal contradiction in neetpg-2013-s1-q0662.** The stem's key answers 20 per cent for the tibia, yet the explanation supplied with it states in the same paragraph that the humerus is 20 per cent, the TIBIA 22 per cent, the femur 27 per cent and the spine 35 per cent. The standard Indian tables give the tibia as 22 per cent; 20 per cent belongs to the humerus. Learn the correct figures and pick the nearest option if the stem recurs.

## Medicine

### Cardiac Arrhythmias & Conduction Disorders

- **`neetpg-2015-s1-q1091`** (AIPGMEE 2015) — *Patient of 1st degree heart block complains of dizziness. Best treatment for this patient is*  
  Stored key: **D. Pacemaker**
  
  **Disagreement with the supplied key, neetpg-2015-s1-q1091.** The key answers 'pacemaker' for a patient with FIRST-degree heart block and dizziness. Isolated first-degree AV block conducts every beat and does not by itself cause dizziness; guidelines recommend pacing only for marked PR prolongation (over 300 ms) producing pseudo-pacemaker syndrome, a Class IIa indication. In a real patient the correct step is to look for another cause of the dizziness and to stop AV-nodal blocking drugs. Of the four options offered, pacing is nevertheless the only definitive therapy, which is how the key was derived.

- **`neetpg-2013-s1-q1870`** (AIPGMEE 2013) — *Read the following ECG. It demonstrates which of the following?*  
  Stored key: **D. Ventricular fibrillation**
  
  **Disagreement with the supplied key, neetpg-2013-s1-q1870.** The explanation calls a rhythm with 'very high rate, irregular, absent P waves and a fibrillatory baseline' ventricular fibrillation, in a patient described as having presented with palpitations. Ventricular fibrillation has NO identifiable QRS complexes and the patient is pulseless and unconscious - a conscious patient with palpitations and an irregular fibrillatory baseline has atrial fibrillation. Judge the strip on QRS morphology and on whether the patient has a pulse, not on the word 'fibrillatory'.

- **`neetpg-2025-s1-q0065`** (NEET PG 2025) — *Q. A patient with atrial fibrillation (AF) and mitral stenosis presents within 4 hours of symptom onset and is hemodynamically stable. What is the nex*  
  Stored key: **B. Cardioversion**
- **`neetpg-2025-s1-q0069`** (NEET PG 2025) — *A patient with an irregular pulse and palpitations. He arrives 2.5 hours after symptom onset. No H/O diabetes, and BP is 160/100 mmHg. What is the nex*  
  Stored key: **A. Control the ventricular rate with verapamil.l**
  
  **Internally inconsistent 2025 recalls, neetpg-2025-s1-q0065 and neetpg-2025-s1-q0069.** One gives cardioversion for stable AF at 4 hours with mitral stenosis, the other gives rate control with verapamil for stable AF at 2.5 hours. The defensible framework is: unstable, shock; stable and under 48 hours, either strategy is acceptable with anticoagulation cover; but in rheumatic mitral stenosis the left atrium is large and thrombus may pre-date the arrhythmia, so many authorities require a TEE before cardioversion even inside 48 hours. Answer such questions on the stability and the duration first.

- **`neetpg-2014-s1-q1439`** (AIPGMEE 2014) — *LBBB is seen with all except*  
  Stored key: **C. Hypokalemia**
  
  **Nuance in neetpg-2014-s1-q1439.** The supplied explanation lists hyperkalaemia and the Ashman phenomenon as causes of LBBB. In fact hyperkalaemia widens the QRS diffusely rather than producing a true LBBB pattern, and Ashman aberration classically takes an RBBB morphology because the right bundle has the longer refractory period. Hypokalaemia is still the right answer to the 'except', since it produces U waves and a long QU rather than any bundle branch block.

### Electrolyte, Acid–Base & Renal Tubular Disorders

- **`neetpg-2014-s1-q1266`** (AIPGMEE 2014) — *1 year old male child is having Heart Rate 40/min, BP 90/60. His serum Potassium = 6.5 what is the next best management?*  
  Stored key: **C. Sodium bicarbonate**
  
  **Disagreement with the supplied key (neetpg-2014-s1-q1266):** a child with potassium 6.5 mEq/L and a heart rate of 40/min has cardiotoxic hyperkalemia, and the correct first drug is **IV calcium (chloride or gluconate)** to stabilise the myocardial membrane. The paper keys sodium bicarbonate. Bicarbonate does shift potassium intracellularly and is a legitimate second step, but with bradycardia already present, calcium comes first in every current protocol including PALS. Write calcium in a clinical answer; be aware the answer key here says bicarbonate.

- **`neetpg-2013-s1-q1276`** (AIPGMEE 2013) — *Which of the following is not feature of distal renal tubular acidosis*  
  Stored key: **A. Normal anion gap**
  
  **Internal contradiction in a supplied key (neetpg-2013-s1-q1276):** the question asks what is NOT a feature of distal RTA and keys 'normal anion gap'. A normal anion gap IS a defining feature of distal RTA. The genuine odd one out among those options is hyperkalemia, since type 1 RTA causes hypokalemia. The supplied explanation itself concedes this. Learn the physiology, not the key.

- **`neetpg-2021-s1-q0109`** (NEET PG 2021) — *30 y/F, 70 kg man with serum sodium=120. Calculate total sodium deficit*  
  Stored key: **B. 840**
  
  **Inconsistent stem (neetpg-2021-s1-q0109):** the stem reads '30 y/F, 70 kg man'. The key uses the male factor, 0.6 x 70 x (140 - 120) = 840 mEq. Using the female factor of 0.5 gives 700 mEq. If a question specifies a woman, use 0.5; here the arithmetic that matches an option is the male one.

### Opportunistic Mycoses: Candida, Cryptococcus, Aspergillus and Mucormycosis

- **`neetpg-2017-s1-q0068`** (AIPGMEE 2017) — *Which fungus is most commonly associated with orbital cellulitis in patients with diabetic ketoacidosis.*  
  Stored key: **C. Aspergillus**
- **`neetpg-2018-s1-q0093`** (AIPGMEE 2018) — *Which fungus is most commonly associated with orbital cellulitis in patients with diabetic ketoacidosis.*  
  Stored key: **C. Aspergillus**
  
  **Disagreement with the supplied key (neetpg-2017-s1-q0068 and neetpg-2018-s1-q0093):** both papers key *Aspergillus* as the fungus most commonly associated with orbital cellulitis in diabetic ketoacidosis. Standard teaching (Harrison, Ananthanarayan, Khurana) makes **Mucorales - Rhizopus - the classic organism in ketoacidosis**, because acidosis liberates transferrin-bound iron that Mucorales require; *Aspergillus* is the mould of neutropenia and steroid use. - **`neetpg-2013-s1-q1641`** (AIPGMEE 2013) — *History of yellow green watery discharge and pruritus?*  
  Stored key: **B. Candida**

- **`neetpg-2013-s1-q0786`** (AIPGMEE 2013) — *The fungus with septate hyphae and dichotomous branching is -*

## Orthopaedics

### Fracture Healing, Nonunion & Fracture Complications

- **`neetpg-2016-s1-q1479`** (AIPGMEE 2016) — *Cubitus valgus develops as complication of -*  
  Stored key: **A. Jupitor fracture**
  
  **Disagreement with a supplied explanation (neetpg-2016-s1-q1479):** the explanation calls a lateral condyle fracture a 'Jupiter fracture'. That eponym is not standard - Jupiter's classification refers to distal humeral and olecranon fracture patterns, and lateral condyle fractures are classified by **Milch**. The fact being tested is sound: **cubitus valgus with tardy ulnar nerve palsy follows a malunited or nonunited lateral condyle fracture**. Learn the fracture, not the eponym.

- **`neetpg-2015-s1-q1475`** (AIPGMEE 2015) — *Greenstick/ Nightstick fractures are seen in -*  
  Stored key: **A. Children**
  
  **Disagreement with a question stem (neetpg-2015-s1-q1475):** the stem groups 'greenstick / nightstick' fractures as occurring in children. A **nightstick fracture is an isolated ulnar shaft fracture in an adult** from a direct blow while warding off a stick. Only the greenstick fracture is paediatric, and that is what makes 'children' the right option.

- **`neetpg-2014-s1-q1778`** (AIPGMEE 2014) — *Most common cause of acute compartment syndrome in children is ?*  
  Stored key: **A. Fracture supracondylar humerus**
  
  **Disagreement with a supplied explanation (neetpg-2014-s1-q1778):** the explanation states that crush injury of the forearm is the commonest cause of acute compartment syndrome in adults. Current teaching (Maheshwari, Apley) makes the **closed tibial diaphyseal fracture** the single commonest cause overall in adults. The keyed answer - supracondylar humerus fracture as the commonest cause **in children** - is correct.

- **`neetpg-2013-s1-q1495`** (AIPGMEE 2013) — *Dignostic sign of fracture-*  
  Stored key: **A. Abnormal mobility at fracture site**
  
  **Disagreement with a supplied explanation (neetpg-2013-s1-q1495):** that explanation lists tenderness among the 'unfailing' signs of fracture. Tenderness is sensitive but occurs in any soft tissue injury; only **abnormal mobility and crepitus** are truly diagnostic. The keyed answer (abnormal mobility) is right.

## Paediatrics

### Paediatric Neurology & Neuromuscular Disorders

- **`neetpg-2012-s1-q1206`** (AIPGMEE 2012) — *Treatment of simple febrile convulsion is based on*  
  Stored key: **A. Control of fever**
  
  **Disagreement with the supplied explanation.** The explanation to neetpg-2012-s1-q1206 states that treating fever spikes reduces the likelihood of febrile seizure recurrence.
