# NEET PG 2026 — How to use this study material

**Exam: 30 August 2026. Study window: 11–29 August, 19 days.**

This document is the map. Read it once, properly, before you open any of the PDFs —
it will change how you use them.

---

## 1. What this material is, and what it is not

**It is** every previous-year question you have, sorted into topics, with a teaching
chapter written for each topic that is built *from those specific questions*.

**It is not** revision notes. A revision note assumes you already know the topic and
just reminds you of the facts. These chapters teach the concept — mechanism,
classification, clinical correlation — and then show you every way the exam has
actually asked about it. You can learn a topic from scratch here.

**It is also not a textbook, and it is machine-written.** Every chapter ends with a
`Standard textbook reference` section naming specific chapters in the standard books.
That section is an instruction, not decoration. Anything that contradicts what you
already know, and in particular **drug doses, numerical cut-offs, and national
programme figures**, should be checked against the book before you commit it to
memory. You have three weeks; a wrong number learned confidently is worse than a gap.

---

## 2. The goal

Not "cover the syllabus". The goal is: **on 30 August, for every question on the
paper, you have already seen that concept tested at least once.**

That is achievable in 19 days only because the exam repeats itself. The material is
organised entirely around exploiting that.

---

## 3. Where the questions came from — and why it matters

Your question bank is not one exam. It is three, and they are not equally useful:

| Exam | Questions | Years | What it is |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **NEET PG** | 1,460 | 2019–2025 | The exam you are sitting |
| **INI CET** | 265 | 2025, May 2026 | AIIMS/PGI/JIPMER/NIMHANS combined entrance |
| **AIPGMEE** | 9,277 | 2012–2018 | NEET PG's direct predecessor — same exam, renamed in 2019 |

Two things follow from this table, and they are the whole reason the ranking works.

**First: 84% of your questions are from AIPGMEE.** If topics were ranked by how often
they appear, the 2014 paper alone — 2,104 questions — would outvote the last three
real NEET PG papers combined. You would spend your best days studying what was
high-yield twelve years ago.

**Second: the recent papers are small.** NEET PG 2024 contributed 110 questions. A raw
count buries them.

So topics are **not** ranked by count. Each topic is scored on its **share of each
paper it appeared in**, and each paper is weighted by how much it predicts your 2026
exam:

| Paper | Weight |
|---|---:|
| NEET PG 2025 | **0.78** |
| NEET PG 2024 | 0.61 |
| INI CET May 2026 | 0.60 |
| INI CET 2025 | 0.47 |
| NEET PG 2020 | 0.22 |
| AIPGMEE 2014 | **0.02** |

Recent NEET PG and INI CET papers decide the ranking. AIPGMEE cannot outvote them,
but it still breaks ties between topics the recent papers happened not to touch —
which is exactly what you want, because those topics are still examinable.

> **A caveat you should know.** NBE does not release NEET PG papers and AIIMS does not
> release INI CET papers. Every paper from 2025 onward is a memory-based recall
> compiled by students. Questions from those papers are marked
> *"memory-based recall"* under the question box. Treat their answer keys as strong
> evidence, not gospel — if a chapter flags a disagreement, believe the chapter's
> reasoning over the key.

## 4. What the numbers on every chapter mean

Each chapter is headed with something like:

> `Tier A | ~1.8 expected questions | 34 previous-year questions`

**`~1.8 expected questions`** is the important one. It is a literal prediction: *this
topic is worth about 1.8 questions on your 200-question paper.* Across all topics
these sum to roughly 200. So a topic marked `~0.2` is worth about a fifth of one
question — read it if you have time, skip it without guilt if you don't.

**Tier** is just that number bucketed, and it controls how much was written:

| Tier | Expected questions | Chapter | Read it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | ≥ 1.0 | Full teaching chapter, ~2,000 words | Always. This is the exam. |
| **B** | 0.4 – 1.0 | Standard chapter, ~1,200 words | Always, if you are on schedule |
| **C** | < 0.4 | Compact fact sheet, ~500 words | If time allows. **This is what you cut.** |

**`trend`** tells you whether a topic is *rising* (recent papers ask it more than the
old ones did) or *falling*. A rising tier-B topic deserves more of your attention than
a falling tier-A one.

## 5. The files, and the order to open them

### Start here — `NEETPG_00_MASTER_INDEX.pdf`
Your navigation document. Contains the top-100 topics ranked across all subjects, the
full topic list for every subject with its expected-question score, and the paper
weight table. **Read the top-100 list on day 1 and again on day 19.** If you did
nothing else, knowing what is on that list would still move your score.

### Then — `NEETPG_00_CALENDAR.pdf`
The 19-day plan, one page per day, with tick boxes and a page reference for every
topic. See §6 for how it is built and how to recover when you fall behind.

### The bulk — `NEETPG_<Subject>.pdf` (one per subject)
The actual study material. Each has a table of contents, and chapters are ordered
**high-yield first within each section** — so if you stop reading a section halfway,
you have already read the part that matters most.

Every chapter contains:

- **Must know** — the bare facts. If you read nothing else in the chapter, read this.
- **Concept sections** — the actual teaching.
- **Tables** — comparisons. This exam loves comparisons; these are the highest-value
  thing on the page.
- **Mnemonics** — standard ones used in Indian medical teaching, not invented ones.
- **Asked again and again** — concepts that appeared in several different papers,
  detected automatically. **This is the single most predictive section in the
  chapter.** A concept asked in three separate years is the best available evidence it
  will be asked a fourth time.
- **Where students lose marks** — the traps, and any place the source explanation
  disagreed with the correct answer.
- **Standard textbook reference** — see §1. Not decoration.
- **Previous-year questions** — every PYQ for that topic, verbatim, with the answer
  marked and the explanation. These are printed from the original data, never rewritten.

### For the last two days — `NEETPG_HIGH_YIELD_ONLY.pdf`
Every tier-A chapter from every subject, in one file, ranked. This is what you carry
on 28–29 August. Nothing else.

## 6. The 19-day plan, and what to do when you fall behind

The calendar is built on four decisions worth understanding, because you will need to
deviate from it and you should know what you are trading away.

1. **Tier A is scheduled first (days 1–9), tier C last (days 10–15).** This is
   deliberate insurance: if you lose three days to illness or fatigue, you lose tier C,
   which is worth the least. **Never "catch up" by skipping forward — the plan is
   already ordered so that falling behind is the cheapest possible failure.**

2. **Three to four subjects every day, not one subject per day.** Blocked study feels
   more organised and retains worse. The real paper mixes subjects and so does this.

3. **Sections stay together.** Related topics land on the same or adjacent days,
   because learning the whole of a section at once is what makes it stick.

4. **A fifth of every day is revisits** — topics from 3 days ago and 9 days ago. This
   is not optional padding. Reading 550 topics once and never again is how you arrive
   on the 30th having forgotten the first week.

**Days 16–19 introduce no new material.** Day 16–17 sweep every tier-A Must-know list;
day 18 is a full 200-question timed mock built from NEET PG 2023–2025 and INI CET
2025–2026; day 19 reviews the mock and the top-100 list.

**If you are more than two days behind by day 10:** stop reading tier C entirely, and
read only the *Must know* and *Asked again and again* sections of tier B. Do not skip
tier A for any reason.

## 7. Bringing doubts back

Every artifact is keyed by two stable ids, and quoting them is the fastest way to get
a precise answer:

- **Topic id** — e.g. `pharmacology.antimicrobial-chemotherapy.antitubercular-drugs`.
  Printed in the topic index and used as the chapter filename.
- **Question id** — e.g. `neetpg-2025-s1-q0042`, `inicet-2026-s1-q0011`. Printed under
  every question box.

So: *"In `pharmacology.…​.antitubercular-drugs`, the Must-know says X but Katzung says
Y — which is right?"* is answerable immediately. "That TB drug question was wrong" is not.

Worth flagging back, in particular:
- anything contradicting your textbook, especially doses and cut-offs
- an answer key you believe is wrong (likely on memory-based 2025–2026 questions)
- a topic that feels mis-scored against your own sense of the exam
- a question tagged to a topic it does not belong in

## 8. Rebuilding any of this

Everything is regenerable from the corpus. From the repository root:

```bash
python3 -m pipeline.taxonomy      --emit | --ingest   # topic taxonomy
python3 -m pipeline.tag_topics    --rules             # free keyword pass
python3 -m pipeline.tag_topics    --emit-packets      # the rest, as work packets
python3 -m pipeline.tag_topics    --ingest
python3 -m pipeline.apply_topics  --dry-run           # inspect before writing
python3 -m pipeline.apply_topics  --commit            # merge topics into the corpus
python3 -m pipeline.build_topic_index                 # scoring and ranking
python3 -m pipeline.chapters      --emit --subject X  # chapter work packets
python3 -m pipeline.chapters      --ingest            # validate and store chapters
python3 -m pipeline.render_pdf    --all --index --high-yield
python3 -m pipeline.build_calendar --hours 10         # change your daily budget
```

`--hours` is the one you are most likely to want: the calendar assumes 10 study hours
a day. If that is wrong, rebuild it with a number you will actually hit, and re-render
the calendar PDF. A plan you follow beats a plan that is optimal.

Every step that writes to the corpus backs it up first (`data/backups/`) and refuses
to write if it would change any field it did not declare, so re-running is safe.
