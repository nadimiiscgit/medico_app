# NEET PG / Med-Ed Competitive Landscape

**Prepared:** August 2026
**Scope:** AMBOSS, Marrow, CoreBTR, Cerebellum Academy, PrepLadder, DBMCI (Bhatia), DAMS, Pre PG
**Purpose:** understand the competitive set, then locate where this app can actually win.

> Pricing below is list/promo price observed in Aug 2026. Every Indian player runs
> near-permanent 20–60% "discounts" off an inflated MRP, so treat MRP as fiction and
> the discounted number as the real price. Revenue figures are from RoC filings via
> Entrackr/Tracxn; user counts are Play Store install buckets and App Store rating
> counts — directionally right, not audited.

---

## 1. The market we are actually in

| Fact | Number |
|---|---|
| NEET PG 2025 registrations | 2,42,493 |
| Candidates who appeared | 2,30,114 |
| Candidates who qualified | 1,28,116 |
| PG seats available (MD/MS/Diploma) | ~73,111 |
| Effective annual paying cohort | ~2–2.5 lakh, plus 3–4 lakh MBBS students preparing 1–3 years ahead |

Two structural facts drive everything else:

1. **The funnel is brutally competitive but small.** ~2.3 lakh test-takers a year for
   ~73k seats. That's a ~3.2:1 crunch — enough anxiety to sustain ₹25,000+ ARPU, but
   the total addressable user base is *tiny* compared to NEET UG or JEE. This is a
   high-ARPU, low-volume market. It rewards depth and brand, not growth hacking.
2. **The buyer is price-insensitive at the top and price-obsessed at the bottom.** A
   final-year MBBS student whose family has already spent ₹50L–₹1Cr on the degree will
   pay ₹27,000 for PrepLadder without blinking. That same student will also pirate
   notes off Telegram. Both behaviours coexist in one person, and the split is
   *by product category*: they pay for video + rank, they refuse to pay for MCQs alone.

That second point is the single most important insight in this document.

**Read the ratings column carefully.** The two market leaders have *mediocre* ratings
(4.2, 4.5) despite charging ₹27–37k. The two cheapest/free products (Pre PG, CoreBTR)
have the *best* ratings (4.7–4.83). Satisfaction and revenue are almost inversely
correlated here. That gap is where an insurgent lives.

## 3. Player-by-player deep dive

### 3.1 Marrow — the profit machine

**What it is.** Full-stack NEET PG prep: 810+ hours of video across 19 subjects,
18,500+ MCQ QBank, 390+ tests including 130+ Grand Tests. Owned by Neuroglia Health
(the DailyRounds entity), which was acquired by Japanese healthcare-media giant **M3 Inc.
in April 2019**.

**Business model.** Pure B2C subscription, tiered by *content bundle* and *duration*:

| Plan | What's in it | 3 mo | 6 mo | 12 mo | 24 mo | 36 mo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A — Test Series | 390+ tests only | — | — | ₹14,999 (12mo) | — | — |
| B — QBank + Tests | 18,500 MCQs + tests | ₹6,999 | ₹11,999 | ₹18,999 | — | — |
| C — Video + QBank + Tests | everything, 810+ hrs | ₹16,999 | ₹28,499 | ₹36,999 | ₹52,999 | ₹64,999 |

**Financials (this is the headline).** Neuroglia Health FY25: **₹641 Cr revenue,
₹363 Cr profit** — a ~57% net margin, with Marrow contributing 88% of operating revenue.
FY24 was ₹568 Cr / ₹320 Cr. This is one of the most profitable ed-tech businesses in
India, and it got there on **$1.8M of lifetime funding**. It is not a startup burning
money — it is a cash-generating asset owned by a listed Japanese parent.

**Moat.**
- *Content depth as sunk cost.* 810 hours of professionally produced video across 19
  subjects is a ~₹50–100 Cr production asset that must be refreshed every 2–3 years.
  Nobody replicates it cheaply.
- *Multi-year lock-in.* The 24/36-month plans capture a student from 2nd-year MBBS
  through their second attempt. Once you've bought 36 months, you don't switch.
- *Brand = "the serious one."* Marrow is understood as the rigorous, evidence-based
  option. Its 4.5 rating on 70K reviews is the highest of the big two.
- *Magic Module / Schema.* Adaptive re-serving of your wrong answers, and a "high-yield
  topic map." These are retention features, not acquisition features — they raise
  switching cost mid-subscription.

**Distribution.** Weak paid marketing, strong word-of-mouth. Their own YouTube channel
is small (~11K subs) — Marrow deliberately doesn't give content away free. Distribution
runs through **college-level peer networks and seniors' recommendations**, which is why
they can spend almost nothing on CAC and still print 57% margins.

**Exact use case.** "I am 3rd/final-year MBBS and I want one product that teaches me the
entire syllabus from zero and tests me." It is a *replacement for coaching class*.

---

### 3.2 PrepLadder — the scale play

**What it is.** The other full-stack platform. Founded 2015 in Chandigarh by the Goyal
brothers, **acquired by Unacademy in July 2020 for ₹374.6 Cr ($50M)**.

**Pricing (Elite plan, Version XI):**

| Duration | MRP | Street price |
|---|---|---|
| 6 mo | ₹23,999 | ₹19,199 |
| 12 mo | ₹33,499 | ₹26,799 |
| 24 mo | ₹45,499 | ₹36,399 |
| 36 mo | ₹55,899 | ₹41,924 |
| 72 mo | ₹79,999 | ₹59,999 |

Plus a cheaper PRO tier (21 mock tests vs. 300+) and long-term *extension* plans
(₹13,500–₹21,000) for existing subscribers — a smart retention/pricing instrument that
monetises the "I didn't crack it this year" moment at a discount instead of losing them.

**Financials.** FY22 was ₹115 Cr revenue on a ₹144 Cr loss. Post-restructuring Unacademy
now describes PrepLadder as cash-flow positive and one of its few working assets.
Unacademy overall: ₹826 Cr income FY25, losses down 31% to ₹436 Cr.

**Moat.**
- *Unacademy's capital and cross-sell* (weaker than it sounds — Unacademy is retrenching).
- *5M+ installs* is the largest footprint in the category, roughly 5x Marrow. Much of
  this is FMGE/NEET-SS/Residency and free-tier users, but top-of-funnel is real.
- *SPARK* — in-app spaced repetition tied to weak areas. Note this: **the market leader
  has already shipped the spaced-repetition feature.** It is table stakes now, not a
  differentiator.
- *Production polish* — "15% shorter" videos, 3D models, cadaveric dissection footage,
  Hinglish + English tracks. They compete on *watchability*.

**Vulnerability.** Teachers have publicly pursued legal action over unpaid dues under
Unacademy ownership. Faculty churn is the soft underbelly of a platform whose value is
faculty-delivered. And a 4.2 rating against ₹27k pricing is not a happy customer base.

**Exact use case.** Same as Marrow — full syllabus replacement — but chosen by students
who find Marrow dry and want faster, more produced video. It's the "Coke vs. Pepsi" of
this market; students genuinely agonise over which of the two to buy, and rarely buy both.

### 3.3 Cerebellum Academy — the faculty cartel

**What it is.** Founded by **five of the most recognised names in Indian PG teaching** —
Dr. Gobind Rai Garg, Dr. Vivek Jain, Dr. Sparsh Gupta, Dr. Praveen Tripathi, Dr. Apurv
Mehra — plus Dr. Zainab Vora, Dr. Dilip Kumar and others. Several of these people wrote
the standard review textbooks students already own.

**Model.** LIVE-first cohort classes ("Mission 3.0"), sold as programs rather than
subscriptions. Durations from 45 days to 60 months; **₹6,510 to ₹64,490**, with optional
printed notes as an upsell. Claims a 90% strike rate from NEET PG 2025.

**Moat.** Pure **personal brand equity**. Students don't buy "Cerebellum," they buy
"Sparsh sir's pharmacology." This is the strongest moat in the category and also the most
fragile — it walks out of the building. It literally did: Dr. Zainab Vora, listed as
Cerebellum faculty, now runs her own competing platform (CoreBTR).

**Weakness.** The technology is bad. 3.7 Play / 3.75 iOS is the worst rating among the
serious players. They are teachers who bought an app, not a product company.

**Distribution.** YouTube 305K subs + heavy live-class FOMO ("Batch 3 starting").
Cohort start dates create urgency that a self-paced product can't.

**Exact use case.** "I need accountability and a live schedule, and I want to be taught
by the person who wrote my textbook." It sells to students who fail at self-paced study.

---

### 3.4 CoreBTR — the solo-creator insurgent (study this one closest)

**What it is.** Dr. Zainab Vora's own revision platform, launched ~2025-26 under
SABRSHUKR COCARE PVT LTD. **Revision only** — not a syllabus-teaching product. 19-subject
high-yield revision: short videos, concise notes, "BTR Pearls," flashcards, mock tests.
Philosophy branding: *Believe, Trust, Revise*.

**Pricing.** ~₹14,920 for 1 year, **including printed workbooks** (Vol I & II + a blank
"Chhoti Copy"). No discount codes, no refunds. Half the price of a Marrow Plan C, and
positioned as a *complement* to it rather than a replacement.

**Why it matters more than its size suggests.** 100K+ installs is small. But
**10,395 iOS ratings at 4.73** — from a product that is roughly a year old — is an
extraordinary engagement-per-install number, better than any incumbent. She converted a
personal following (~60K YouTube, large Instagram/Telegram) into a paying product
directly, bypassing every platform.

**Moat.** Creator trust + the revision layer being genuinely under-served. Also the
**physical workbook** — a printed artifact is unpirateable, creates a daily habit surface,
and justifies price in a way pixels don't.

**Exact use case.** "I've already done my first pass with Marrow/PrepLadder. It's 60 days
to the exam and I'm drowning. Give me the structured last-mile revision." That is a
distinct, urgent, high-willingness-to-pay job — and it is the second purchase, not the first.

---

### 3.5 DBMCI / Dr. Bhatia — the legacy brand in decline

**What it is.** Dr. Bhatia Medical Coaching Institute, one of the two original offline
NEET PG coaching brands. Digital presence split across two apps — **eGurukul** (500K+
installs) and the newer **DBMCI One** (100K+ installs).

**Pricing.** By far the cheapest of the institutional players: plans at **₹3,866 (3+2 mo)
to ₹6,875 (12+2 mo)**, LMRP revision points at ₹895, with 25–60% coupon codes flying
around constantly.

**Health check — this is a brand in trouble.** eGurukul: 4.2 on Play but **2.97 on iOS
across 2,304 ratings**. DBMCI One: 3.1. Running two competing apps and migrating content
between them ("NEET PG and FMGE videos transition to DBMCI One") is a classic sign of an
organisation that can't decide what it is. The YouTube channel has 520K subs but only
19 published videos — a legacy audience being under-served.

**Moat.** Residual brand recall among older students and the offline classroom network.
Eroding fast. Competing on price is what you do when you've lost the ability to compete
on product.

**Exact use case.** Price-driven purchase, or legacy loyalty from students whose seniors
went to Bhatia's classroom.

---

### 3.6 DAMS — the biggest offline brand

**What it is.** Delhi Academy of Medical Sciences. The other original offline giant.
Classroom-first, now hybrid: recorded lectures, live two-way interactive classes, 70+
"TND" (Test & Discussion) mentorship sessions, 5-level testing, QBank with video solutions.

**Pricing.** ~**₹84,000 for a 2-year online learning program** — the most expensive
offering in this document, because it's priced like coaching, not like software.

**Reach.** 1M+ app installs (4.2, 25.8K reviews) and **949K YouTube subscribers — the
largest owned audience of anyone here.**

**Moat.** Physical footprint (Karol Bagh + national centres), the largest free-content
audience, and 25+ years of "DAMS = PG coaching" recall among parents as much as students.

**Weakness.** Priced for a classroom economy in a market that has moved to ₹27k apps.
Their 949K YouTube audience is enormous and almost certainly under-monetised — that's
their asset and their unsolved problem.

**Exact use case.** "My family wants me in a proper coaching institute with attendance
and mentors." It sells structure and supervision, often to the parent as much as the student.

---

### 3.7 Pre PG — the freemium outlier (our closest structural analogue)

**What it is.** An indie, free-first QBank app run by **MCQdb LLC**. 75,000+ topic-wise
MCQs, 21,000+ clinical questions, PYQs with explanations, free daily test series, plus
**StudyPal** (AI doubt-solving) and **PrepDNA** (topic-level weakness analytics). Claims a
96.8% strike rate for NEET PG 2025.

**Model.** Freemium. Enormous free tier, premium upsell. No video production, no faculty
salaries, no studio — which is exactly why it can give away 75K questions.

**Performance.** 500K+ installs, **4.7 on Play (35.5K reviews) and 4.83 on iOS (8,227
ratings)** — the highest-rated product in the entire category, by a distance, beating
products that cost ₹37,000.

**Why this matters to us.** Pre PG proves the thesis that **a content-light, question-heavy,
free-first app can win real love and real scale in this market without video or faculty.**
It is the existence proof for our category. It is also our most direct competitor, and it
is 5 years ahead of us on content volume and has already shipped AI doubt-solving.

**Exact use case.** "I can't afford ₹27k, or I've already bought videos and just want
volume practice and PYQs on my phone for free."

---

### 3.8 AMBOSS — the different animal

**What it is.** A Berlin-founded (2012) global **medical knowledge platform**: a
1,400+ article peer-reviewed clinical library fused with a 13,900+ question Qbank,
covering USMLE Step 1/2/3 *and* point-of-care clinical use.

**Scale.** 1M+ professional users across 180 countries, 500+ employees including
**150+ in-house physicians**. Raised **€240M in March 2025** (KIRKBI, M&G, Lightrock) and
converted to a European SE. Revenue estimated around $105M. "Every second inpatient
treatment in Germany is carried out by a physician supported by AMBOSS."

**Pricing.**

| Tier | Price |
|---|---|
| Student monthly | $19.99/mo |
| Student yearly | $12.50/mo ($150/yr) |
| Qbank Bundle | $448/yr |
| Student Life (through PGY-1) | $1,199 one-time |
| Clinician monthly / yearly | $29.99 / $21.58 per mo |

**Moat — and this is the one worth learning from.** AMBOSS is the only player here whose
moat is *structural rather than personal*:
- **Editorial infrastructure.** 150 salaried physicians continuously maintaining a
  cross-linked knowledge graph. Every Qbank explanation links into library articles; every
  article links to relevant questions. That takes a decade to build and can't be bought.
- **Dual-market lifecycle.** Student → resident → practising clinician on one platform.
  Indian players lose the customer the day the exam ends; AMBOSS keeps them for 30 years.
- **Institutional sales.** Universities and hospitals buy site licences, which makes
  adoption a default rather than a choice.

**Relevance to India.** Low, directly — it's USMLE/clinical, not NEET PG, and $150–448/yr
in a market where the free option is genuinely good. It matters to Indian students only
for **USMLE aspirants and FMGE-abroad students**. But strategically it's the most
instructive company in this list: it shows that in med-ed, **the durable asset is
structured, interlinked, physician-maintained content — not video, and not a personality.**

## 4. Cross-cutting patterns

### 4.1 Four archetypes, four different businesses

| Archetype | Who | Sells | Real moat | Fragility |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Full-stack platform | Marrow, PrepLadder | Syllabus replacement | Sunk video cost + multi-year lock-in | Content refresh treadmill |
| Faculty cartel / live | Cerebellum, DAMS | Structure + a named teacher | Personal brand, cohort FOMO | Teachers leave (see: CoreBTR) |
| Solo-creator | CoreBTR | The revision layer | Direct audience trust | One person, one channel |
| Freemium QBank | Pre PG | Volume practice, free | Cost structure + goodwill | Hard to monetise |

### 4.2 The price ladder is remarkably clean

```
₹0            Pre PG free tier, YouTube, Telegram piracy
₹3,866–6,875  DBMCI (discounting to survive)
₹6,999        Marrow QBank-only, 3 months  ← the real floor for paid MCQs
₹14,920       CoreBTR (revision layer + print)
₹19,000       Marrow QBank+Tests, 12 months
₹26,799       PrepLadder Elite 12 mo      ← the market's centre of gravity
₹36,999       Marrow Plan C 12 mo
₹64,490       Cerebellum 60-month
₹84,000       DAMS 2-year program
```

Two things jump out. First, **there is a hole between ₹0 and ₹6,999**, and nobody
credible occupies it. Second, **the tests-only tier prices higher than the QBank-only
tier** (Marrow: ₹14,999 for tests vs ₹18,999 for QBank+tests over 12 months) — which
tells you that in this market, *ranked assessment is worth more than content*. Students
pay for the number that tells them where they stand.

### 4.3 What people actually pay for — in order

1. **A rank percentile against a real cohort.** Grand Tests with 20,000 co-participants.
   No amount of solo practice substitutes for "you are at the 84th percentile."
2. **A trusted human voice** that compresses the syllabus.
3. **Structure** — a schedule that removes the burden of deciding what to study today.
4. **Last-mile revision** in the final 60 days.
5. **Question volume.** Dead last. Everyone has 18,500–75,000 MCQs. It is a commodity,
   and Pre PG gives it away.

### 4.4 Distribution channels, ranked by what they actually deliver

| Channel | Who uses it | Effectiveness |
|---|---|---|
| Senior→junior word of mouth | Marrow (dominant) | Highest. Free CAC, and why Marrow prints 57% margins |
| YouTube free content | DAMS 949K, DBMCI 520K, PrepLadder 70K, Cerebellum 305K, AMBOSS 354K | High reach, weak conversion for the legacy brands |
| Instagram/Telegram creator following | CoreBTR, Cerebellum faculty | Highest conversion per follower |
| Campus ambassadors / college reps | All Indian players | Steady, high-touch |
| Coupon/affiliate ecosystem (medicalcoupons.in, neetfmgeplans.com) | Marrow, PrepLadder, DBMCI, Cerebellum | An entire parasitic industry exists on these discount codes — evidence that list prices are theatre |
| App store organic | Pre PG | The only player really winning here, on the back of ratings |
| Institutional/library sales | AMBOSS only | Nonexistent in India; a genuine white space |

## 5. Where we sit

### 5.1 What we actually have today

| Asset | Detail |
|---|---|
| NEET PG PYQs | **10,541 questions, 2012–2024**, all 13 years, with explanations for every one |
| Practice bank | **117,003 MCQs** across 20 subjects (MedMCQA-derived, Apache-2.0 — commercially usable) |
| Spaced repetition | Real SM-2 (Anki algorithm) on flashcards |
| Analytics | Per-subject accuracy, streaks, progress |
| Study tools | Notes, bookmarks, revision notes, question-ID deep links |
| Delivery | Offline-first PWA — no install friction, works on any phone |
| Monetisation | Pro unlock via redeem codes (client-side, localStorage) |
| Cost structure | Static JSON on Firebase Hosting. Marginal cost per user ≈ ₹0 |

### 5.2 Honest gap analysis

| Dimension | Leaders | Us | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Video | 810+ hrs | none | Not winnable, and not worth trying |
| Faculty brand | Household names | none | Not winnable alone — but *partnerable* |
| Ranked test series | 130+ Grand Tests, pan-India percentile | none | **The single biggest gap. Requires a cohort, not code.** |
| QBank volume | 18.5K curated | 117K (noisy) + 10.5K clean PYQ | We win on raw volume, lose on curation |
| PYQ archive | Bundled inside QBank | 13 years, clean, explained, ID-addressable | **We are best-in-class here and nobody markets it** |
| Spaced repetition | PrepLadder SPARK, Marrow Magic Module | SM-2, arguably better | Parity — no longer a differentiator |
| Accounts / sync | Full | **localStorage only, device-locked** | **Blocking. Kills retention and any real monetisation.** |
| Entitlement security | Server-verified | SHA-256 hashes in the client bundle | Trivially bypassed. Fine for a friends-and-family MVP, not for revenue |
| Distribution | 1M–5M installs, 950K YouTube subs | zero | **The actual problem. Not the product.** |
| Content quality | Physician-reviewed | MedMCQA is community-scraped and noisy | Real risk — medical wrong answers destroy trust permanently |

### 5.3 The honest summary

**We are a well-built free PYQ + practice app in a market where the money is in video,
faculty, and rank.** Our closest competitor is Pre PG — same shape, same economics —
and it is five years and 500,000 installs ahead of us, with AI doubt-solving already
shipped and the highest ratings in the category.

The good news: the two things we do best are things the incumbents treat as a
*by-product*. Nobody sells a clean, complete, explained 13-year PYQ archive as a first-class
product — it's buried inside a ₹27,000 bundle. And nobody has built the revision/retention
layer as a standalone product except CoreBTR, which proved in under a year that it works
and priced it at ₹14,920.

The bad news: our differentiators are features, not moats. SM-2 is a weekend of work for
a competitor. What can't be copied in this market is (a) a decade of physician-maintained
interlinked content, à la AMBOSS, (b) a personal audience, à la CoreBTR, or (c) a testing
cohort large enough that its percentile means something. **We have none of the three, and
a strategy that doesn't end in acquiring one of them is not a strategy.**

## 6. Where to play — three options

### Option A: The PYQ authority (recommended)

Stop being "another NEET PG app" and become **the definitive NEET PG previous-year
question resource**. Every student in India solves PYQs, all of them, every year. It is
the one universally agreed-upon study activity. Today they do it inside an expensive
bundle or off a bad PDF.

- Own the positioning: *"Every NEET PG question from 2012 to 2024. Explained. Free. Offline."*
- Go deep where bundles are shallow: year-wise and subject-wise slicing, repeat-concept
  tagging (which concepts recurred across years — this is the thing students actually
  want and nobody does well), difficulty trends, per-question discussion.
- Free forever on PYQ. Monetise the layer on top: adaptive revision, custom tests,
  analytics, printable revision sheets. **₹499–₹1,999/year**, an order of magnitude
  below the incumbents, priced as a supplement rather than a replacement.
- This is a *complement* to Marrow/PrepLadder, not a competitor. That's a feature —
  the addressable market becomes everyone, including their paying customers.

### Option B: Creator white-label

CoreBTR is the proof: a single faculty member with 60K YouTube subs built a
₹15,000/year product with 10,000+ five-star ratings in about a year. There are dozens of
mid-tier medical educators with 20K–200K followers and no product, because building one
is hard.

**We have the product and no audience. They have the audience and no product.** Offer the
platform white-labelled, revenue share. This converts our biggest weakness into someone
else's solved problem. It is the fastest realistic path to revenue.

### Option C: Go up-market against the bundle

Build video, hire faculty, run a ranked test series. **Do not do this.** It requires
₹10–50 Cr, competes head-on with a ₹641 Cr profitable incumbent owned by a listed Japanese
parent and a ₹374 Cr-acquired platform, and every advantage we have (zero marginal cost,
no faculty payroll, offline delivery) becomes irrelevant the moment we enter that game.

## 7. Concrete next moves

**Fix before any growth push** — these are correctness/viability issues, not features:

1. **Accounts + cloud sync.** localStorage means a student who changes phones loses
   everything. In a 12-month study cycle that is a guaranteed churn event, and it makes
   any subscription unsellable. Firebase Auth + Firestore; the project is already on Firebase.
2. **Server-verified entitlements.** The current redeem-code hashes ship in the JS bundle.
   Acceptable for a trusted MVP audience (and the code comments say so honestly), but it
   must move server-side before a paid launch.
3. **Content QA on the 117K MedMCQA questions.** They are community-scraped and noisy.
   A single confidently-wrong medical explanation costs more trust than 1,000 right ones
   earn. Either flag them clearly as "community practice bank" and separate them from the
   verified PYQ set, or run an LLM+reviewer pass and publish the accuracy rate.

**Then, to differentiate:**

4. **Repeat-concept mapping across 13 years of PYQs.** "This concept has been asked 7
   times since 2012." Nobody sells this, our data uniquely supports it, and it is exactly
   what a student in the final 60 days wants.
5. **Weekly ranked mock with a live cohort.** Even 500 participants produces a percentile,
   and percentile is what students pay for. Start small; the cohort is the moat.
6. **2025 and 2026 papers.** The archive stops at 2024. Currency is the whole proposition.

**On distribution — pick one and commit:**

7. Either seed hard through college WhatsApp/Telegram groups with the free PYQ archive as
   the hook (the Pre PG playbook), or pursue Option B and let a creator bring the audience.
   Doing neither is the default failure mode, and it has nothing to do with how good the
   app is.
